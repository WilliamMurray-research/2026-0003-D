# The Evolutionary Neuroarchitecture of Human Intelligence
## Cortical–Subcortical Dynamics, Firing Manifolds, and the Roots of Human Irrationality

William Murray  
16 August 2026  

## **Abstract**  
Human cognition emerges from the interaction between evolutionarily ancient subcortical systems and the newer cortical structures layered above them. This paper synthesizes current neuroscientific understanding of how subcortical firing manifolds shape, constrain, and modulate cortical population dynamics, producing both adaptive intelligence and characteristic forms of human irrationality. I argue that human cognition is best understood as a hierarchically integrated dynamical system, where ancient motivational circuits set the operating modes within which cortical computation unfolds. This architecture explains the persistence of tribalism, fear‑based biases, and emotional irrationality in modern environments, and provides a biologically grounded template for brain‑inspired artificial general intelligence.

---

## **1. Introduction**
Human intelligence is not solely a product of the neocortex. Instead, it arises from the structured interaction between evolutionarily old and new neural systems. The cortex contributes flexible computation, abstraction, and planning, while subcortical structures provide motivation, action selection, emotional salience, and survival‑oriented heuristics. Understanding this interaction requires analysing the firing manifolds — low‑dimensional trajectories of neural population activity — that characterize both cortical and subcortical dynamics.

This paper synthesizes current knowledge about these systems and their interactions, drawing on research in systems neuroscience, computational neurobiology, and evolutionary psychology. It also highlights how these principles can inform the design of biologically grounded artificial general intelligence.

## **2. Evolutionary Architecture of the Human Brain**

### **2.1 Subcortical Systems: The Ancient Substrate of Agency**
Subcortical structures — including the basal ganglia, amygdala, hypothalamus, brainstem, and cerebellum — constitute the evolutionarily ancient core of the vertebrate brain. These systems implement fundamental survival functions such as:

- action selection  
- motivational drives  
- reward shaping  
- threat detection  
- autonomic regulation  

These circuits generate agency rather than flexible computation. They determine what the organism wants, how it prioritizes stimuli, and which actions are permitted.

### **2.2 Cortical Systems: The Evolutionarily New Computational Layer**
The cortex, especially the association cortex, provides the computational machinery for:

- abstract reasoning  
- long‑term planning  
- symbolic representation  
- multimodal integration  
- voluntary motor control  

The cortex computes; the subcortex decides.

## **3. Cortical–Subcortical Interaction: The Core of Human Cognition**

### **3.1 Thalamo‑Cortical Loops: Setting the Computational Mode**
The thalamus regulates cortical firing manifolds by gating sensory input, modulating oscillatory rhythms, and controlling attentional state. These loops define the geometry of cortical population dynamics and determine which sensory information reaches higher‑order processing.

### **3.2 Basal Ganglia–Cortical Loops: Selecting Manifolds**
Basal ganglia circuits determine which cortical trajectories are allowed to unfold. Dopamine adjusts the probability of entering specific manifolds, implementing biological policy gating. This mechanism enables reinforcement learning and goal‑directed behavior.

### **3.3 Amygdala and Hypothalamus: Emotional Biasing of Manifolds**
Emotional states reshape cortical attractor landscapes. Fear compresses cortical manifolds into reactive, low‑dimensional modes. Reward expands exploratory manifolds. Stress reduces flexibility and increases rigidity. These biases explain why emotional states often override rational computation.

---

## **4. Firing Manifolds: A Dynamical Systems View of Cognition**

### **4.1 Cortical Manifolds**
Cortical activity forms high‑dimensional attractor landscapes. These manifolds encode sensory representations, motor plans, abstract concepts, and predictive models. They are flexible, context‑dependent, and shaped by learning.

### **4.2 Subcortical Manifolds**
Subcortical manifolds are lower‑dimensional and more rigid, reflecting conserved survival functions. They encode basic drives, threat responses, and action selection patterns.

### **4.3 Interaction**
Subcortical systems reshape cortical attractors, alter transition probabilities, modulate stability and chaos, and gate access to specific manifolds. In effect, subcortex sets the rules of the game, and cortex plays within those rules.

## **5. Evolutionary Mismatch and Human Irrationality**

### **5.1 Tribalism**
Humans evolved in small groups; modern societies exceed the scale our social circuits can handle. This produces in‑group bias, out‑group distrust, and heightened sensitivity to status.

### **5.2 Fear Bias**
Ancient threat‑detection circuits trigger disproportionate responses to modern social threats. The amygdala reacts faster than cortical reasoning can intervene.

### **5.3 Reward Mismatch**
Dopamine systems optimized for scarcity are hijacked by modern stimuli such as sugar, novelty, social media, and status signals. This produces compulsive behaviors and distorted valuation.

### **5.4 Rationalization**
The cortex often acts as a post‑hoc justification engine, rationalizing decisions made by subcortical systems. This creates the illusion of rationality even when behavior is driven by ancient emotional circuits.

These mismatches explain why humans are systematically irrational in predictable ways.

## **6. Implications for AGI Design**

### **6.1 Minimal Biological AGI Architecture**
A biologically grounded AGI requires more than perception, motor output, and abstract reasoning. It must integrate:

- perception  
- action selection  
- working memory  
- long‑term memory  
- reward systems  
- predictive world models  
- planning  

Motor–sensory–association cortex alone is insufficient; agency requires subcortical analogues.

### **6.2 The Cortex–Subcortex Blueprint**
The biological architecture suggests AGI should integrate motivational subsystems, policy‑gating modules, hierarchical predictive models, and emotion‑like salience systems. This yields an agent rather than a passive pattern recognizer.

---

## **7. Conclusion**
Human intelligence emerges from the structured interaction between ancient subcortical systems and the newer cortical layers. Subcortical firing manifolds constrain and bias cortical dynamics, producing both adaptive behavior and characteristic irrationality. Understanding this architecture provides a foundation for biologically inspired AGI systems that integrate motivation, action selection, and flexible computation.
