## ABSTRACT

Bangladesh faces accelerating hydrological pressures driven by monsoon variability, glacial melt, riverbank erosion, and coastal salinity intrusion. Nature-Based Solutions (NbS) offer a cost-positive, community-scalable strategy for mitigating these risks, but their effectiveness depends on precise spatial placement, continuous monitoring, and adaptive management. This paper specifies the national digital-twin architecture that integrates the NbS governance framework of Part 1, the reinforcement learning optimisation engine of Part 2, and the operational engineering platform of the HRL technical specification into a unified national system.

The architecture operates across three interdependent layers: a **digital layer** of satellite telemetry, hydrological models, ecological baselines, and financial-risk indicators; a **computational layer** of hierarchical RL agents, directed HydroECA optimisation, Ensemble Kalman Filter data assimilation, and Asynchronous Parallel Vectorised Monte Carlo Tree Search; and a **community execution layer** of Union Parishads, cooperatives, and farmer groups who validate, correct, and implement digital-twin outputs. The Satellite-Enabled Agricultural Risk Engine (SARE) provides the high-frequency, spatially explicit risk indices required to populate the RL state tensor. A financial-sector feedback loop — in which HydroECA gains reduce credit risk indices, reduce bank provisioning requirements, and reduce NbS borrowing costs — strengthens the economic rationale for sustained programme investment.

The digital twin is not a planning substitute for community governance. It is a closed-loop system in which digital modelling and physical intervention are continuously reconciled through community observation, Bayesian data assimilation, and human-in-the-loop oversight protocols. Together, these components constitute a national infrastructure for spatial optimisation, adaptive management, governance coordination, and climate-finance integration across Bangladesh's delta system.

**Keywords:** digital twin, nature-based solutions, Bangladesh, satellite telemetry, SARE, HydroECA, reinforcement learning, Ensemble Kalman Filter, community governance, climate finance

## 1. INTRODUCTION

Bangladesh's hydrological landscape is undergoing rapid, multidirectional change. Flooding, drought stress, salinity intrusion, and morphodynamic erosion impose recurrent economic losses across agriculture, banking, insurance, and public finance. The national NbS framework developed in Part 1 of this series proposes a structured response: community-governed ecological intervention across a 25-year phased programme. The RL optimisation engine developed in Part 2 provides the spatial planning layer that determines where, in what sequence, and in what configuration those interventions should be placed to maximise hydrological connectivity benefit.

Both frameworks, however, require an underlying data infrastructure that neither specifies: a continuously updated, spatially explicit representation of Bangladesh's hydrological, agricultural, ecological, and socio-political state that can feed the RL environment, track intervention outcomes, support governance coordination, and generate the financial-risk indicators required to unlock climate-finance flows. This is the role of the national digital twin.

A digital twin in this context is not a static model or a one-time planning tool. It is a closed-loop system that continuously ingests satellite telemetry, field observations, community reports, and financial data; updates a dynamic state representation; generates optimised intervention recommendations; and feeds observed outcomes back into the model to improve future predictions. The digital twin does not replace community governance — Union Parishads, cooperatives, and farmer groups remain the operational layer through which digital-twin outputs become physical interventions. It enhances governance by providing evidence-driven guidance, transparent accountability, and real-time adaptive management capacity.

This paper specifies the architecture of that system, integrating the NbS governance framework (Part 1), the RL optimisation engine (Part 2 v0.2), and the HRL technical specification into a unified national infrastructure.

## 2. THREE-LAYER ARCHITECTURE

The digital twin operates across three interdependent layers. No layer is sufficient alone; the system's value derives from the closed loop between them.

```
┌─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
│                         DIGITAL LAYER                               │
│  Satellite Telemetry · SARE Risk Indices · Hydrological Models      │
│  Ecological Baselines · Agricultural Indicators · Financial Data     │
└────────────────────────────────┬────────────────────────────────────┘
                                 │ EnKF Assimilation
                                 ▼
┌─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
│                      COMPUTATIONAL LAYER                            │
│  14-Channel State Tensor · Directed HydroECA · APV-MCTS             │
│  Hierarchical RL Agents · HITL Risk Escalation · Reward Engine      │
└────────────────────────────────┬────────────────────────────────────┘
                                 │ Intervention Recommendations
                                 ▼
┌─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
│                   COMMUNITY EXECUTION LAYER                         │
│  Union Parishads · Cooperatives · Farmer Groups · Field Teams       │
│  Validation · Correction · Implementation · Observation Reporting   │
└────────────────────────────────┬────────────────────────────────────┘
                                 │ Community Observations (Bayesian weighted)
                                 ▼
                    [Returns to Digital Layer via EnKF]
```

The loop is closed: digital twin outputs drive community action; community action changes the landscape; community observation reports the change; EnKF assimilation updates the digital twin. This is the operational logic of the entire system.

## 3. THE DIGITAL LAYER

### 3.1 Satellite Telemetry

Satellite-derived indices provide continuous, objective, spatially granular measurements of hydrological and agricultural conditions across Bangladesh. The core telemetry streams feeding the digital twin are:

- **Sentinel-1 SAR (6-day repeat):** Flood extent mapping, seasonal inundation depth, embankment breach detection. Primary input for Channel 3 (Seasonal Inundation Depth) and Channel 12 (Hydro-meteorological Flood Risk Index).
- **Sentinel-2 Optical (5-day repeat):** Agricultural land classification, crop phase identification, wetland and mangrove canopy cover. Primary input for Channels 4 and 6.
- **Drone LiDAR (Monthly, targeted):** High-resolution morphodynamic change detection, riparian vegetation density, channel geometry. Primary input for Channel 13 (Morphodynamic Volatility Rate) and model validation.
- **UAV surveys (Campaign-based):** Embankment condition, char land mapping, intervention outcome assessment.

### 3.2 The Satellite-Enabled Agricultural Risk Engine (SARE)

SARE is the primary data backbone of the digital twin. It transforms multi-sensor satellite telemetry into high-frequency, spatially explicit risk indices that map directly onto hydrological and ecological channels of the RL state tensor.

SARE resolves the largest technical gap in the RL-NbS synthesis identified in Part 2: the availability of spatially explicit, high-frequency hydrological resistance data required for directed HydroECA calibration. Without SARE, resistance layer construction relies on periodic field campaigns and interpolation, introducing substantial lag and spatial uncertainty. SARE provides near-continuous resistance layer updates at the resolution required to keep the state tensor current between field measurement cycles.

SARE's core output indices and their tensor channel mappings are:

| SARE Index | Description | Primary Channel Target |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Flood Extent Index | Inundated area fraction per cell, monsoon phase | Ch 3: Seasonal Inundation Depth |
| Soil Moisture Volatility | Coefficient of variation of soil moisture, rolling 90-day window | Ch 1: Soil Permeability / Infiltration |
| Drought Stress Index | Standardised precipitation-evapotranspiration deficit | Ch 12: Hydro-meteorological Flood Risk |
| Salinity Intrusion Index | Surface water EC proxy from spectral indices | Ch 5: Soil & Surface Water Salinity |
| Erosion Hazard Index | Change detection from multi-temporal SAR coherence | Ch 13: Morphodynamic Volatility Rate |
| Crop Rotation Stability | Temporal consistency of cropping pattern classification | Ch 4: Agricultural Land Classification |
| Canopy Cover Change | NDVI-derived vegetation density change rate | Ch 6: Wetland & Mangrove Canopy Cover |

SARE's implementation phases align with RL environment development, with full tensor channel coverage required by Month 14 of the research agenda (Phase B completion, Part 2 v0.2).

### 3.3 Hydrological Modelling

National hydrological models simulate river discharge, floodplain connectivity, sediment transport, and monsoon dynamics. These models support two functions within the digital twin: (i) generation of the background forecast state vector fed into the EnKF assimilation engine at each timestep; and (ii) scenario testing for proposed NbS interventions, enabling planners to simulate hydrological outcomes before physical implementation.

The hydrological models are not embedded within the RL training loop — this would be computationally prohibitive. Instead, they are emulated within the loop by the reduced-order neural hydrological transition models running on the GPU accelerator pipeline (Section 4.4). The full hydrological models operate on the outer planning cycle, updating the emulator's training data at quarterly intervals.

### 3.4 Ecological Baselines

Ecological baselines encode the pre-intervention state of vegetation cover, wetland extent, mangrove density, and biodiversity indicators. These baselines constitute the initial state of the digital twin at Phase 1 launch and serve as the counterfactual against which HydroECA gains are measured.

### 3.5 Agricultural and Financial Indicators

Agricultural baselines track crop yields, irrigation demand, and land-use patterns at Union Parishad level. Financial indicators track non-performing loan rates, insurance payouts, and district-level credit exposure from the banking and insurance sectors.

These indicators serve two functions: (i) they feed the co-benefit and salinity penalty components of the RL reward function; and (ii) they constitute the measurement basis for the financial-sector feedback loop formalised in Section 7.

### 4.1 State Tensor

The physical and socio-political state of the delta is discretised into a 14-channel state tensor $\mathbf{S}_t \in \mathbb{R}^{H \times W \times 14}$. The canonical channel assignments — adopted from the HRL technical specification and superseding the 13-channel numbering in Part 2 v0.2 — are:

| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |

| Ch 1 | Soil Permeability & Infiltration Rate | mm/hr | Soil Cores / SARE |
| Ch 2 | Embankment & Hard Infrastructure Mask | Binary | Satellite / UAV |
| Ch 3 | Seasonal Inundation Depth | m | Sentinel-1 / Telemetry |
| Ch 4 | Agricultural Land Classification & Crop Phase | Categorical | Sentinel-2 / SARE |
| Ch 5 | Soil & Surface Water Salinity | dS/m / ppt | In-situ Probes / SARE |
| Ch 6 | Wetland & Mangrove Canopy Cover | % | LiDAR / SARE |
| Ch 7 | Freshwater Storage Capacity | m³ | Hydrological Gauge |
| Ch 8 | Governance Feasibility Index (GFI) | [0, 1] | Quarterly Field Surveys |
| Ch 9 | Land Ownership & Dispute Density | Count/Area | Land Cadastral Records |
| Ch 10 | Labour & Nursery Material Availability | [0, 1] | Agricultural Audits |

**Note on channel reconciliation:** Part 2 v0.2 used a 13-channel specification with GFI at Channel 7 and a different ordering for hydrological variables. The HRL specification's 14-channel layout is adopted here as canonical. Part 2 v0.2 channel references should be read with this mapping in mind; a reconciliation table is provided in Appendix A.

### 4.2 Directed HydroECA

The RL agent's primary optimisation target — Hydrological Equivalent Connected Area (HydroECA) — is defined on a directed, asymmetric graph to reflect the anisotropic physics of water flow. The formal definition, developed in Part 2 v0.2 Section 3.2, is:

$$\text{HydroECA} = \sqrt{\sum_{i=1}^{n} \sum_{j=1}^{n} a_i \, a_j \, p_{ij}^*}$$

where $a_i$, $a_j$ are the storage/attenuation capacities of nodes $i$ and $j$, and $p_{ij}^*$ is the directed transmission probability of water flow from node $i$ to node $j$:

$$p_{ij}^* = \exp\left(-\theta \cdot R_{ij}^*\right)$$

with $R_{ij}^*$ the least-resistance directed path cost and $\theta$ a calibration parameter fit to BWDB flood routing observations.

Critically, $p_{ij}^* \neq p_{ji}^*$: transmission probability is asymmetric. Under monsoon flood conditions, backwater effects can reverse effective connectivity for reaches that are unidirectional under normal flow. HydroECA is therefore computed on two season-specific directed graphs:

$$\text{HydroECA}_\text{monsoon} \quad \text{and} \quad \text{HydroECA}_\text{dry}$$

The RL reward function combines both with programme-specified weights reflecting the relative priority of flood attenuation versus dry-season water availability. Transitional period resistance parameterisations are interpolated between the two seasonal graphs.

Channel 0 of the state tensor encodes a normalised HydroECA value for each spatial cell, updated by the Graph Analysis Engine at each timestep as the directed resistance graph evolves through satellite-derived channel updates and intervention outcomes.

### 4.3 EnKF Data Assimilation Pipeline

State tensor channels are continuously updated via an Ensemble Kalman Filter (EnKF) executing $N_\text{ens} = 100$ parallel state ensemble members.

```
      FIELD TELEMETRY STREAMS                    EnKF ASSIMILATION ENGINE
┌─────────────────────────────────┐        ┌──────────────────────────────────┐
│ • Pressure Transducers (Hourly) │        │ 1. Background Forecast (M)       │
│ • EC Salinity Probes (Daily)    │───────►│ 2. Gaspari-Cohn Localised Gain   │
│ • Sentinel-1/2 SAR (6-Day)      │        │    (cutoff radius r = 15 km)     │
│ • Drone LiDAR (Monthly)         │        │ 3. Analysis State Update (x_i)   │
│ • Community Observations        │        │ 4. Adaptive Variance Inflation   │
└─────────────────────────────────┘        └────────────────┬─────────────────┘
                                                            │
                                Reshaped 14-Channel Tensor  │
                                                            ▼
                                               ┌────────────────────────┐
                                               │   GPU VRAM Ring Buffer  │
                                               └────────────────────────┘
```

The Kalman Gain $\mathbf{K}_{t,\text{loc}}$ is localised using a Gaspari-Cohn correlation function with cutoff radius $r = 15\,\text{km}$ to eliminate spurious long-distance covariances across hydrologically isolated sub-basins. Dynamic variance inflation $\lambda_t \geq 1.0$ is applied to the analysed anomaly matrix before writing the mean state tensor to VRAM, preventing ensemble collapse under sustained low-innovation periods.

#### 4.3.1 Bayesian Weighting of Community Observations

Community-generated observations — flood depth estimates, soil condition assessments, crop stress reports, fish catch records — enter the EnKF assimilation pipeline as an additional observation stream. They are not treated as equivalent to instrument-grade telemetry. A Bayesian uncertainty weighting scheme assigns observation error variances to community inputs based on: measurement type (quantitative estimate vs. qualitative classification); observer training level (Union Parishad field officer vs. untrained farmer group member); and historical agreement rate between community reports and satellite-derived ground truth for the same cell and variable.

The observation error covariance matrix $\mathbf{R}_t$ is block-structured to reflect these differential uncertainties:

$$\mathbf{R}_t = \text{diag}\left(\mathbf{R}_t^\text{instrument}, \, \mathbf{R}_t^\text{community}\right)$$

where $\mathbf{R}_t^\text{community}$ has larger diagonal entries (higher observation uncertainty) than $\mathbf{R}_t^\text{instrument}$, with magnitudes calibrated from Phase D pilot data. This ensures that community observations contribute meaningfully to state updates while being appropriately down-weighted relative to telemetry in proportion to their uncertainty.

The assimilation framework returns interpretable feedback to community observers: when a community report is substantially down-weighted because it conflicts with satellite evidence, the discrepancy is flagged to the field team for ground-truth investigation rather than silently absorbed into the ensemble.

### 4.4 Hierarchical RL and APV-MCTS Compute Architecture

The decision engine implements a two-level Hierarchical RL (HRL) architecture to manage the state-space complexity of delta-wide optimisation.

```
┌─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
│                  MACRO-AGENT (Sub-Basin Scale)                      │
│  • Spatial: 200 × 300 grid at 1 km² resolution                     │
│  • Horizon: 5–10 years (seasonal steps)                             │
│  • Task: Continuous inter-basin NbS budget allocation vectors (b_k) │
│  • PUCT: c_puct = 1.25 · Rollout budget: 1,600 simulations/step    │
└──────────────────────────────┬──────────────────────────────────────┘
                               │ Target boundary vectors (b_k)
                               ▼
┌─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
│                  MICRO-AGENT (Reach / Plot Scale)                   │
│  • Spatial: 100 × 100 grid at 100 m² resolution                    │
│  • Horizon: 1 year (monthly steps)                                  │
│  • Task: Discrete physical placement and local governance actions   │
│  • PUCT: c_puct = 2.50 · Rollout budget: 800 simulations/step      │
└─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┘
```

The three-order-of-magnitude resolution jump between macro and micro levels (1 km² to 100 m²) requires careful boundary condition transmission protocol to prevent artefacts at the macro-micro interface. Resolution parameters are adopted provisionally from the HRL specification and require stress-testing during Phase B; intermediate resolution tiers should be evaluated if boundary artefacts are significant.

APV-MCTS runs on a 128-core CPU worker thread pool with atomic compare-and-swap tree traversal and virtual loss injection ($v = 8$ micro, $v = 3$ macro). Batched policy and value network inferences and reduced-order neural hydrological transition model evaluations execute on four enterprise-grade GPUs ($\geq 96\,\text{GB}$ VRAM each) connected via NVLink at 900 GB/s. The full infrastructure specification is documented in the HRL technical baseline.

## 5. THE COMMUNITY EXECUTION LAYER

### 5.1 Communities as Operational Partners, Not Passive Recipients

The digital twin does not replace community governance. Union Parishads, cooperatives, and farmer groups are the operational layer through which digital-twin recommendations become physical interventions. This is not a rhetorical commitment to participation — it is a technical requirement. The closed-loop architecture depends on community observation as a data source, community implementation as the intervention mechanism, and community governance legitimacy as the prerequisite for GFI values high enough to enable physical action.

The community execution layer performs four operational functions:

**Validation.** Communities confirm whether digital predictions match real conditions — flood depth, soil moisture, erosion hotspots, crop stress. This validation is the primary quality-control mechanism for the digital layer's satellite-derived indices. SARE indices calibrated on spectral reflectance may misclassify land use or inundation depth under cloud cover, smoke haze, or unusual spectral conditions; community field confirmation detects and flags these classification errors.

**Correction.** When satellite-derived indices misclassify land use or hazard exposure, communities provide corrective inputs that enter the EnKF assimilation pipeline as observation updates. This prevents model drift — the progressive divergence between the digital twin's state representation and real-world conditions that occurs when discrepancies are not corrected.

**Implementation.** Communities implement high-value NbS interventions identified by the RL agent: riparian buffer planting, wetland restoration, canal maintenance, and agro-ecological water-retention systems. The GFI (Channel 8) encodes the current implementability of interventions in each spatial cell; the community execution layer is the mechanism through which GFI values are earned and maintained.

**Feedback.** Community observations of intervention outcomes — crop yield changes, flood depth changes, erosion indicators, fish catch levels — feed back into the digital twin through the EnKF assimilation pipeline. This closes the loop:

> Digital twin → community action → landscape change → community observation → EnKF update → digital twin

### 5.2 Community Interface Requirements

The digital twin must generate outputs accessible to community users with varying levels of technical literacy. District-level dashboards present HydroECA performance indicators, GFI maps, and intervention priority rankings in formats designed for use by Upazila administrators and Union Parishad chairpersons. Community-level interfaces present localised flood risk forecasts, recommended intervention actions, and yield-risk co-benefit estimates in formats accessible to farmer groups and cooperative members.

Community observation reporting interfaces must be low-bandwidth, mobile-compatible, and functional during monsoon-season connectivity constraints. Data entry protocols must be designed in co-production with Union Parishad representatives to ensure adoption and data quality.

## 6. GOVERNANCE SAFEGUARDS AND HITL PROTOCOLS

### 6.1 Risk Escalation Matrix

Operational risks are managed through continuous monitoring of Channel 13 (Morphodynamic Volatility Rate, $\sigma^\text{morph}$) and Channel 8 (GFI). The two-stage escalation protocol operates as follows:

```
┌─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
│  STAGE 1: YELLOW ALERT (Advisory)                                   │
│  Trigger: σ_morph ∈ [0.65, 0.85)  OR  GFI ∈ (0.25, 0.40]          │
│  Action: 3× w_morph penalty injection; 72-hr field audit alert      │
└──────────────────────────────────┬──────────────────────────────────┘
                                   │ Breach severity escalation
                                   ▼
┌─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
│  STAGE 2: RED ALERT (Emergency Fail-Safe)                           │
│  Trigger: σ_morph ≥ 0.85  OR  GFI ≤ 0.25                           │
│  Action: Physical action masking P(s, a_physical) = 0; 24–48hr HITL│
└─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┘
```

Stage 2 automated action masking zeros all physical action vectors directed at cell $u$ within the MCTS selection layer. The tree search engine automatically backpropagates a topology re-route, setting node capacity $\alpha_u = 0$ and shifting capital budget allocations to secondary candidate nodes within the sub-basin.

### 6.2 De-Escalation Gate

A cell is unmasked and returned to the MCTS action pool only after satisfying two conditions simultaneously:

1. **Telemetry Stability:** Channel 13 volatility $\sigma_u^\text{morph} < 0.50$ for 14 consecutive days.
2. **Dual Sign-off:** Digital authorisation co-signed by the District Hydrological Engineer and Community Governance Lead.

This dual-trigger de-escalation prevents premature return to action in geomorphologically active cells and ensures that community governance endorsement is required alongside technical clearance.

### 6.3 Manual Override Logging

All human-in-the-loop overrides are cryptographically signed and immutably logged, providing an auditable record of every departure from RL-generated recommendations. Override events include the triggering channel value, operator identity, override type (advisory clearance or permanent abandonment), and justification text. This log constitutes the accountability record for parliamentary and climate-finance reporting.

### 6.4 GFI Endogeneity Safeguard

As identified in Part 2 v0.2 Section 3.3.2, the RL agent's recommendations will tend to concentrate community engagement resources in already-accessible cells, reinforcing high-GFI cells and starving strategically important low-GFI cells of governance investment. The GFI exploration bonus introduced in Part 2 v0.2 addresses this at the reward function level. The HITL protocol adds a second safeguard: quarterly governance pipeline audits that flag any sub-basin in which no low-GFI high-HydroECA cells have received governance investment in the preceding 90 days. Such cells trigger a Yellow Alert on the GFI dimension, prompting a 72-hour review of community engagement sequencing.

## 7. THE FINANCIAL-SECTOR FEEDBACK LOOP

### 7.1 Structure of the Loop

The financial-sector feedback loop is the mechanism by which NbS hydrological gains translate into reduced credit risk and lower NbS borrowing costs, strengthening the long-term economic rationale for sustained programme investment.

The chain operates as follows:

```
HydroECA gains
      │
      ▼
SARE risk indices decline
(flood exposure, erosion hazard, salinity intrusion)
      │
      ▼
Agricultural yield volatility declines
(reduced crop loss from flooding and salinity)
      │
      ▼
Non-performing loan rates decline in NbS districts
(agricultural borrower creditworthiness improves)
      │
      ▼
Bank provisioning requirements decline
(Bangladesh Bank regulatory capital requirements linked to district NPL rates)
      │
      ▼
Agricultural lending rates decline in NbS districts
      │
      ▼
NbS adoption costs decline for smallholder borrowers
      │
      ▼
NbS programme uptake increases
      │
      [Returns to HydroECA gains]
```

### 7.2 Measurement Architecture

Each link in the chain requires a specified measurement mechanism within the digital twin:

- **HydroECA → SARE indices:** Direct: SARE indices are computed from satellite telemetry that captures the hydrological consequences of NbS interventions. HydroECA gain in a sub-basin predicts a measurable decline in SARE flood and erosion indices within 1–3 monsoon seasons.
- **SARE indices → agricultural yield volatility:** Measured via Sentinel-2 crop classification time series combined with district-level yield survey data from the Department of Agricultural Extension.
- **Yield volatility → NPL rates:** Measured via Bangladesh Bank district-level NPL reporting, matched to NbS and control districts in the Phase D DiD framework.
- **NPL rates → provisioning and lending rates:** Measured via bank-level regulatory filings. Requires a data-sharing agreement with Bangladesh Bank to link district NPL outcomes to bank-level provision adjustments.
- **Lending rates → NbS adoption:** Measured via cooperative loan uptake records and GFI field survey data.

### 7.3 Climate Finance Integration

The financial feedback loop creates the measurement architecture required for climate-finance instruments: green bonds, results-based climate finance, and parametric insurance products linked to HydroECA performance. HydroECA gains, validated against SARE indices and the Phase D synthetic control framework, constitute a verifiable results chain that satisfies the additionality and attribution requirements of most climate-finance standards. This is the mechanism by which the digital twin supports climate-finance integration beyond the domestic programme budget.

## 9. PHASE D VALIDATION PROTOCOL

### 9.1 Experimental Design

Phase D evaluates the platform across a 24-month field deployment in 16 coastal Union Parishads: 8 treatment UPs receiving RL-guided NbS interventions and 8 control UPs receiving standard district masterplan interventions, separated by a 5 km buffer zone to minimise spatial spillover.

### 9.2 Synthetic Control and Difference-in-Differences

This framework resolves the counterfactual attribution requirement identified in Part 2 v0.2 Section 7.4. For each treatment UP $i$, a synthetic control $i^*$ is constructed via optimal donor weighting $W^*$ minimising pre-intervention outcome divergence (parallel trends verified at $p < 0.01$). Treatment effects are estimated dynamically across the 24-month post-intervention window using difference-in-differences:

$$\hat{\tau}_{it} = (Y_{it} - Y_{i,t_0}) - (Y_{i^*,t} - Y_{i^*,t_0})$$

where $Y_{it}$ is the outcome indicator (HydroECA, SARE salinity index, agricultural yield) for UP $i$ at time $t$, and $t_0$ is the intervention start date.

### 9.3 Phase D Exit Gates

Full spatial rollout (Phase E) requires clearance of four quantitative gates over the 24-month testing window:

1. **HydroECA Gain:** Realised network connectivity in treatment units must exceed synthetic controls by $\geq 18\%$ ($p < 0.01$).
2. **Model Accuracy:** RMSE between predicted and observed HydroECA must remain $< 12\%$.
3. **Governance Pipeline Health:** Zero treatment cells remain trapped in GFI exploration deadlock without physical deployment beyond 9 months.
4. **Salinity Mitigation:** Agricultural soil salinity in designated protection zones must show a statistically significant reduction of $\geq 2.5\,\text{dS/m}$ during dry-season peak.

Gate 3 directly tests the GFI exploration mechanism specified in Part 2 v0.2 Section 3.3.2: if the exploration bonus is correctly calibrated, no high-HydroECA low-GFI cell should remain without governance investment for more than 9 months.

## 10. LIMITATIONS

### 10.1 SARE Index Validity Under Extreme Events

SARE indices derived from optical satellite imagery degrade under heavy cloud cover, smoke haze, and flood-season conditions — precisely when hydrological risk is highest. Sentinel-1 SAR provides cloud-penetrating flood extent data, but its temporal resolution (6-day repeat) may miss rapid flood onset events. The EnKF assimilation framework is designed to handle observation gaps through ensemble propagation, but sustained observation outages during peak monsoon periods will increase ensemble uncertainty and degrade state tensor accuracy during the period of highest operational relevance.

### 10.2 Boundary Condition Transmission in the HRL Architecture

The three-order-of-magnitude resolution jump between macro and micro agents is the principal unvalidated assumption in the computational architecture. If boundary artefacts at the macro-micro interface cause the micro-agent to receive systematically biased budget allocations, micro-level intervention placement will be suboptimal regardless of micro-agent performance. Phase B stress-testing of the boundary condition protocol is a prerequisite for Phase C agent training.

### 10.3 Financial Feedback Loop Dependency on Institutional Cooperation

The financial feedback loop (Section 7) depends on data-sharing agreements with Bangladesh Bank and cooperation from commercial lenders to link district NPL outcomes to bank-level provision adjustments. These agreements are not guaranteed and may face institutional resistance. The digital twin should be designed to deliver value through its hydrological and community governance functions independently of the financial loop, treating the loop as a high-value add-on rather than a system dependency.

### 10.4 Community Interface Adoption Risk

The digital twin's closed-loop integrity depends on sustained community observation reporting. If Union Parishad representatives and farmer groups do not adopt the reporting interfaces — because they are too technically demanding, too time-consuming, or insufficiently trusted — the community observation stream will degrade, the Bayesian assimilation framework will lose its most spatially granular data source, and the GFI update mechanism will lose field-level currency. Interface co-design with community representatives at each phase is a system-critical requirement, not an optional participation exercise.

## 11. CONCLUSION

The national digital-twin architecture specified in this paper completes the three-paper series by providing the data infrastructure, computational engine, and community integration protocol that the NbS governance framework (Part 1) and RL optimisation engine (Part 2) require to function as an operational national system.

The architecture's three layers — digital, computational, and community execution — are mutually dependent. Satellite telemetry without computational optimisation produces maps without recommendations. Computational optimisation without community execution produces recommendations without outcomes. Community execution without digital feedback produces interventions without adaptive management. The value of the system is the closed loop, not any individual component.

Three technical innovations of the series are integrated here for the first time as a unified architecture: the **directed HydroECA metric** (Part 2 v0.2 Section 3.2), which correctly represents the anisotropic physics of delta hydrology; the **GFI with exploration mechanism** (Part 2 v0.2 Section 3.3.2), which prevents governance-driven spatial concentration of intervention; and the **Phase D synthetic control attribution framework** (Section 9), which provides the counterfactual measurement basis required for adaptive management and climate-finance reporting.

Bangladesh's hydrological crisis operates on timescales that will not wait for sequential development of these components. The phased architecture is designed to generate value from the first months of deployment — community interfaces and SARE indices operational in Phase 1, RL optimisation guiding Phase 2 spatial expansion, climate-finance instruments enabled by Phase D attribution data — while the full system matures over the programme's 25-year horizon.

The digital twin does not solve Bangladesh's hydrological challenges. It provides the infrastructure through which Bangladesh's communities, institutions, and financial systems can coordinate a solution.
